Ship-to-Shore Data Exchange

Understand how ships, ports, shipping companies, terminals, and maritime authorities exchange digital information to coordinate vessel arrivals, port services, cargo operations, and administrative procedures.

Maritime Digitalization Port Communication Data Exchange Maritime Single Window
01 / Introduction

Understanding Ship-to-Shore Data Exchange

Maritime operations involve continuous coordination between vessels and organizations located ashore. These organizations may include port authorities, terminal operators, shipping companies, ship agents, customs administrations, and maritime service providers.

Ship-to-Shore Data Exchange refers to the digital transfer and sharing of information between ships and shore-based systems.

The information exchanged may relate to vessel identity, estimated arrival times, cargo declarations, crew details, port service requests, operational updates, and departure procedures.

Core Concept

Ship-to-shore data exchange connects maritime stakeholders through digital information flows. Its purpose is to improve coordination, reduce repeated paperwork, and support informed operational decisions.

02 / Stakeholders

Who Participates in Data Exchange?

Ship-to-shore communication involves several organizations, each responsible for different parts of a port call.

Ships and Ship Operators

Provide vessel information, operational updates, voyage details, and required arrival or departure information.

Port Authorities

Coordinate port access, vessel clearance, port procedures, and relevant regulatory information.

Terminal Operators

Exchange information about berth planning, cargo handling, terminal readiness, and operational schedules.

Ship Agents

Act on behalf of shipowners or operators in many port-related administrative and coordination activities.

Customs Authorities

Receive relevant cargo and customs declarations to support regulatory procedures and risk assessment.

Maritime Service Providers

Coordinate services such as pilotage, towage, mooring, bunkering, and waste reception.

03 / Data Categories

What Information Is Exchanged?

The type of information depends on the vessel's activities, the port's requirements, and the services being coordinated.

Vessel Information

Vessel name, identification numbers, flag, dimensions, registration details, and other required particulars.

Arrival and Departure Data

Estimated, requested, planned, and actual arrival and departure times, depending on the relevant process.

Cargo Information

Cargo declarations, cargo descriptions, container information, and relevant dangerous goods details.

Crew and Passenger Information

Required crew and passenger declarations submitted through the applicable reporting procedures.

Port Service Information

Requests and updates related to pilotage, towage, berthing, mooring, and other port services.

Operational Updates

Changes to schedules, service status, port call events, and relevant operational notifications.

Data Quality Matters

Incorrect vessel details or outdated arrival information can affect several organizations. Reliable exchange requires accurate data, clear responsibilities, and timely updates.

04 / Communication Process

How Does Ship-to-Shore Exchange Work?

A typical digital exchange process begins when information is prepared by an authorized party. It is then transmitted to the relevant shore-based system, checked, processed, and shared with authorized recipients.

01

02

03

04

05

Authorized stakeholders access information

Step 1: Data Generation

Information may originate from the vessel's operational systems, shipping company records, cargo documentation, or an authorized reporting party such as a ship agent.

Step 2: Data Preparation

The information is organized according to the relevant reporting requirements. This may include checking mandatory fields, identifiers, dates, units, and document formats.

Step 3: Digital Transmission

Information is sent through an appropriate communication channel or digital interface. The exact technology depends on the participating systems and the exchange requirements.

Step 4: Processing and Validation

The receiving system may validate the message, check its structure, identify the sender, and determine whether the information can be accepted for further processing.

Step 5: Distribution and Use

Authorized organizations receive the relevant information and use it for administrative, regulatory, or operational purposes.

05 / Technologies

Technologies Used for Data Exchange

Ship-to-shore exchange can use different communication technologies. No single method is suitable for every type of information or every operational situation.

Satellite Communication

Enables communication over long distances and supports data connectivity between vessels and shore-based services.

Mobile Networks

Cellular connectivity can support data exchange when vessels operate within suitable network coverage.

Electronic Data Interchange (EDI)

Allows structured business and administrative information to be exchanged between compatible information systems.

Application Programming Interfaces

APIs allow software systems to exchange structured information through defined requests and responses.

Secure Web Portals

Provide authorized users with interfaces for submitting declarations, reviewing information, and tracking procedures.

Port Digital Platforms

Connect participating organizations through shared services for port-related information and coordination.

Important Distinction

Satellite communication and mobile networks are communication channels. EDI and APIs describe methods or structures for exchanging information between software systems. They operate at different levels of the digital communication process.

06 / Maritime Single Window

Understanding the Maritime Single Window

A Maritime Single Window (MSW) is a digital system through which required information related to a ship's arrival, stay, and departure can be submitted through a single point of entry.

The system supports the electronic exchange of information between reporting parties and relevant public authorities. Information can then be coordinated or distributed to the appropriate authorities according to their responsibilities.

Why Is It Important?

  • Reduces the need to submit the same information repeatedly.
  • Supports electronic submission of required declarations.
  • Improves coordination between public authorities.
  • Helps streamline ship clearance procedures.
  • Supports more consistent digital information exchange.

International Regulatory Context

The International Maritime Organization's Convention on Facilitation of International Maritime Traffic (FAL Convention) provides standards and recommended practices related to formalities and procedures for ships, people, and cargo.

Amendments adopted in 2022 entered into force on 1 January 2024. They made it mandatory for public authorities to establish, maintain, and use single-window systems for the electronic exchange of required information concerning ships' arrival, stay, and departure.

The system is also intended to support the submission of information once and its reuse to the maximum extent possible.

Key Term: Submit Once, Reuse

The single-window approach aims to reduce repeated data submission. It does not mean every organization has unrestricted access to every record.

07 / Port Community Systems

Port Community Systems (PCS)

A Port Community System (PCS) is a digital platform that facilitates the exchange of information between participants in a port community.

Depending on its design and governance, a PCS may connect shipping companies, terminals, freight forwarders, customs, transport operators, and other port-related organizations.

Typical Functions

Information Coordination

Supports the exchange of relevant information between organizations involved in port activities.

Cargo Coordination

Helps participating organizations coordinate information related to cargo movement and handling.

Transport Coordination

Supports the exchange of information needed to coordinate different transport activities.

Digital Documentation

Helps reduce fragmented communication and repeated manual handling of relevant documents.

MSW vs. PCS

Aspect Maritime Single Window Port Community System
Main focus Regulatory information exchange with public authorities Information exchange across port community participants
Typical participants Reporting parties and relevant public authorities Shipping, terminal, logistics, and other port stakeholders
Primary objective Streamline required reporting and clearance procedures Improve coordination of port and logistics processes
Relationship Regulatory reporting framework May exchange information with other port systems, including an MSW
Do Not Confuse the Two

MSW and PCS are related digital port concepts, but they are not interchangeable. Their scope, governance, and participating organizations can differ.

08 / Port Call Coordination

Digital Port Call and Just-in-Time Arrival

A port call involves several activities that must be coordinated before, during, and after a vessel's visit to a port.

Typical Port Call Activities

  • Arrival planning and estimated time updates.
  • Berth availability and berth planning.
  • Pilotage and towage coordination.
  • Mooring and unmooring arrangements.
  • Cargo handling and terminal operations.
  • Bunkering and other vessel services.
  • Departure planning and operational reporting.

Estimated, Requested, Planned, and Actual Times

Digital port call coordination may involve several types of timestamps. They represent different stages of planning and execution.

Term Meaning
ETA Estimated Time of Arrival
RTA Requested Time of Arrival
PTA Planned Time of Arrival
ATA Actual Time of Arrival
ETD Estimated Time of Departure

Just-in-Time Arrival

Just-in-Time (JIT) arrival is an operational approach in which a vessel adjusts its arrival planning and speed, where safe and feasible, to arrive when the berth and required services are expected to be ready.

Better information exchange can support this approach by helping vessels, ports, terminals, and service providers coordinate their plans.

Operational Benefit

Better coordination may reduce unnecessary waiting and support more efficient use of fuel and port resources. JIT arrival must always remain subject to safe navigation, weather, traffic, and operational constraints.

09 / Standards

Data Standards and Interoperability

Different maritime organizations may use different information systems. Without compatible data structures and definitions, exchanging information can become difficult.

IMO Compendium

The IMO Compendium on Facilitation and Electronic Business provides a harmonized reference data model and data set to support the exchange and understanding of maritime information.

Electronic Data Interchange (EDI)

EDI allows organizations to exchange structured information electronically using agreed message formats and data definitions.

APIs and Structured Data

APIs can provide software-to-software interfaces for requesting and sending structured data. They can support event notifications, status updates, and operational coordination.

Why Common Definitions Matter

  • Reduce ambiguity in exchanged information.
  • Improve compatibility between systems.
  • Support consistent interpretation of data.
  • Reduce unnecessary manual re-entry.
  • Improve the reliability of digital workflows.
10 / Cybersecurity

Securing Ship-to-Shore Data Exchange

Digital information exchange creates important operational benefits, but it also introduces cybersecurity and information-protection risks.

Potential Risks

Unauthorized Access

An attacker may attempt to access information or services without appropriate authorization.

Data Manipulation

Unauthorized changes to vessel, cargo, or operational information can affect decisions and coordination.

Data Interception

Poorly protected communication may expose sensitive information to unauthorized parties.

Service Disruption

Cyber incidents may interrupt digital reporting or information exchange services.

Security Measures

  • Use appropriate authentication and access controls.
  • Protect data during transmission and storage.
  • Apply role-based authorization.
  • Maintain audit logs and monitor suspicious activity.
  • Validate received data and its source.
  • Maintain secure backups and recovery procedures.
  • Prepare incident response and business continuity plans.
Security Principle

Successful transmission does not prove that information is authentic or accurate. Systems must consider confidentiality, integrity, availability, authentication, and authorization.

11 / Practical Application

A Vessel Arriving at an Egyptian Port

Consider a cargo vessel preparing to arrive at an Egyptian port. Several organizations need relevant information before the vessel can complete its port procedures.

Stage 1: Preparing Information

The ship's reporting party or authorized ship agent prepares the required vessel, arrival, crew, and cargo information.

Stage 2: Electronic Submission

The required declarations are submitted through the applicable Maritime Single Window procedures.

Stage 3: Information Distribution

The digital system supports the distribution of submitted information to the relevant port systems and authorities according to their responsibilities.

Stage 4: Port Coordination

The relevant organizations use the information for their respective procedures and coordinate the vessel's port call activities.

Egyptian Maritime Single Window

Egypt's Maritime Single Window was announced by the Maritime Transport and Logistics Sector. The sector stated that actual operation began on 15 April 2024 for the electronic submission of ship arrival and departure information.

The Egyptian platform is available through the official portal: Egypt Maritime Single Window .

What Does This Example Demonstrate?

Ship-to-shore exchange is not only a communication technology. It is also an organized process involving data, people, institutions, regulations, and digital systems.

12 / Challenges

Challenges in Maritime Data Exchange

Different Data Formats

Organizations may use systems with different data structures and message formats.

Incomplete Information

Missing or inaccurate information may delay processing and create operational difficulties.

Connectivity Limitations

Network coverage, satellite availability, and technical failures can affect data transmission.

Cybersecurity Threats

Digital systems require protection against unauthorized access, manipulation, and disruption.

Organizational Differences

Stakeholders may follow different procedures, responsibilities, and operational schedules.

System Integration

Connecting legacy systems with newer digital platforms can require additional technical work.

13 / Future Developments

The Future of Maritime Data Exchange

Artificial Intelligence

AI may help analyze operational data, identify unusual patterns, and support planning decisions.

Real-Time Data Sharing

Better exchange of timely information can improve coordination between ships and shore-based stakeholders.

Digital Port Platforms

More connected platforms can support coordinated information exchange across port and logistics activities.

Predictive Planning

Historical and operational information may help estimate delays, service requirements, and resource needs.

These technologies can improve maritime coordination, but their value depends on reliable information, compatible systems, appropriate security, and clear operational responsibilities.
