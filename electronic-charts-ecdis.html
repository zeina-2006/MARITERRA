Smart Navigation & Digital Maritime Navigation / Topic 02

Electronic Charts & ECDIS

Understand how Electronic Navigational Charts and Electronic Chart Display and Information Systems support voyage planning, route monitoring, navigational awareness, and safer maritime operations.

TOPIC 02 Digital Navigation Advanced Maritime Systems
01 / Foundations

Understanding Electronic Charts

Electronic charts are digital representations of maritime geographical and navigational information. They provide information about coastlines, water depths, navigation channels, hazards, landmarks, and maritime infrastructure.

What Is an Electronic Navigational Chart?

An Electronic Navigational Chart (ENC) is an officially produced digital chart containing navigational information. It is created under the authority of a government Hydrographic Office or another authorized organization.

Unlike a simple digital picture of a paper chart, a vector ENC stores information as structured geographic objects. Each object can have a position, geometry, classification, and associated attributes.

For example, a lighthouse can be represented as a geographic object with attributes describing its name, light characteristics, and other relevant information.

Raster Charts vs. Vector Charts

Raster Navigational Charts (RNC)

Raster charts are digital images of existing paper charts. They preserve the visual appearance of the original chart and are displayed as a fixed image.

Their information is not normally stored as individually selectable geographic objects in the same way as vector charts.

Electronic Navigational Charts (ENC)

Vector charts store navigational information as structured objects. Users and compatible systems can select objects, inspect attributes, and control the display of different information layers.

ECDIS can use ENC data for safety-related functions, including certain route-checking and warning functions.

Feature Raster Chart Vector ENC
Data structure Pixel-based chart image Structured geographic objects
Object selection Generally limited Individual objects can be selected
Display control Mostly fixed chart image Flexible information layers
Safety checking Limited compared with vector ENC functions Supports ENC-based safety checking

Important distinction: A raster chart is not automatically equivalent to an ENC. Their data structures and the functions available to navigation systems are different.

What Information Does an ENC Contain?

ENC data can contain multiple categories of navigational information, including:

  • Bathymetry: Depth information and underwater terrain features.
  • Coastlines: Shore boundaries and coastal geographical features.
  • Navigation aids: Buoys, beacons, lights, and other aids to navigation.
  • Hazards: Wrecks, obstructions, rocks, and dangerous underwater features.
  • Navigation routes: Channels, traffic separation schemes, and restricted areas.
  • Landmarks: Structures and geographical objects useful for navigation.
  • Port information: Harbours, terminals, berthing areas, and maritime facilities.

The available information depends on the chart's coverage, production quality, scale, survey data, and update status. An electronic chart should never be assumed to contain every detail about the surrounding maritime environment.

02 / System Architecture

What Is ECDIS?

ECDIS is a navigation information system that combines electronic chart data with navigational sensor inputs and software functions to support voyage planning and monitoring.

Electronic Chart Display and Information System

ECDIS stands for Electronic Chart Display and Information System. It is a specialized maritime navigation system designed to display official electronic navigational charts and support navigational tasks.

ECDIS can combine chart information with the vessel's position, heading, speed, route, and other sensor information. This allows the bridge team to monitor the ship in relation to charted geographical features and navigational hazards.

Main ECDIS Components

1. Display Unit

Presents chart information, vessel position, planned routes, navigational objects, alerts, and system information to the bridge team.

2. ECDIS Software

Processes chart data, manages display settings, supports route planning, and performs safety-related monitoring functions.

3. ENC Database

Stores electronic chart information and applicable chart updates used by the ECDIS.

4. Navigation Sensors

Supply information such as position, heading, and speed. The quality and reliability of these inputs directly affect navigation.

5. Alarm and Monitoring Functions

Provide warnings and indications related to selected safety settings, route monitoring, and system conditions.

6. Backup Arrangements

Provide an independent means of navigation if the primary ECDIS becomes unavailable, according to applicable requirements and vessel arrangements.

How ECDIS Receives Navigation Information

ECDIS may receive data from several onboard systems through suitable interfaces and communication networks.

  • GNSS receiver: Supplies geographical position and timing information.
  • Gyrocompass: Supplies heading information.
  • Speed log: Supplies vessel speed information.
  • Radar: Can provide radar information or an overlay when properly integrated.
  • AIS: Supplies information about transmitting vessels and other AIS targets.
  • Echo sounder: Provides measured water depth beneath the vessel.

Engineering concept: ECDIS does not independently measure every aspect of vessel movement. It depends on external data sources. A wrong sensor input can therefore produce a misleading display even when the chart itself is correct.

ECDIS vs. ECS

An Electronic Chart System (ECS) is a broad term for systems that display electronic chart information. However, not every ECS meets the IMO performance standards required for an approved ECDIS.

A system being able to display an electronic map does not automatically make it suitable for meeting a vessel's statutory chart-carriage requirements.

03 / International Standards

ENC and ECDIS Standards

International standards help ensure that electronic navigational data is structured, displayed, protected, exchanged, and used consistently across compatible maritime systems.

International Hydrographic Organization (IHO)

The International Hydrographic Organization (IHO) develops standards and specifications that support the production and use of hydrographic information, including electronic navigational charts.

IHO S-57 — Transfer Standard for Digital Hydrographic Data Defines the traditional data structure and encoding used for exchanging digital hydrographic information, including S-57 Electronic Navigational Charts.
IHO S-52 — Chart Content and Display Aspects of ECDIS Specifies important aspects of how chart information is presented in ECDIS, including symbols, colours, and display conventions.
IHO S-63 — Data Protection Scheme Defines a protection framework used for the secure distribution and handling of ENC data.
IHO S-101 — Electronic Navigational Chart Product Specification Defines the next-generation ENC product specification developed within the IHO S-100 framework.
IHO S-100 — Universal Hydrographic Data Model Provides a framework for developing and exchanging a wider range of digital hydrographic and maritime information products.

The Transition Toward S-100

Traditional S-57 ENCs remain important in current maritime operations. The S-100 framework introduces a more flexible approach that supports multiple types of marine information products.

These products can include electronic navigational charts, bathymetric surfaces, water-level information, surface currents, and navigational warnings.

This approach can help navigation systems combine different maritime data products in a more integrated environment.

Do not confuse the standards: S-57 and S-101 describe ENC data specifications. S-52 addresses chart presentation, while S-63 addresses data protection. They perform different roles within the electronic navigation ecosystem.

IMO Performance Standards

The International Maritime Organization (IMO) establishes performance standards for ECDIS equipment. These standards describe system capabilities and operational requirements.

IMO Resolution MSC.232(82) adopted revised performance standards for ECDIS in 2006. Later standards also address ECDIS operating with S-100-based information products.

The applicable performance standard depends on the equipment, its approval and installation history, and the relevant regulatory requirements.

04 / Voyage Planning

Route Planning Using ECDIS

Voyage planning is the process of selecting and evaluating a safe route between departure and destination while considering navigational hazards, vessel characteristics, environmental conditions, and operational restrictions.

Route Planning Process

  1. Define the voyage: Identify the departure point, destination, expected operating conditions, and voyage requirements.
  2. Review chart coverage: Confirm that appropriate ENC coverage is available for the complete route.
  3. Identify hazards: Examine shallow waters, wrecks, obstructions, restricted areas, and navigational constraints.
  4. Create waypoints: Define the intended route using a sequence of geographical positions.
  5. Configure safety parameters: Set appropriate safety values based on the vessel's characteristics and operating conditions.
  6. Perform route checking: Use available ECDIS safety-checking functions to identify potential conflicts between the planned route and charted information.
  7. Review and approve: The responsible navigation officers examine the route before it is used for the voyage.

Important ECDIS Safety Settings

Safety Contour

Defines the depth boundary used by ECDIS to distinguish between waters considered safe and unsafe according to the selected settings.

Safety Depth

Highlights depth information relevant to the vessel's selected safety criteria. It helps officers identify potentially dangerous depth areas.

Shallow Contour

Represents a selected shallow-water boundary and contributes to the chart's depth presentation.

Deep Contour

Provides a depth boundary that helps distinguish deeper areas from shallower water on the chart.

Cross-Track Distance

Defines the permitted distance from the planned route before an indication or alarm may be generated.

Look-Ahead Function

Examines a selected area ahead of the vessel in relation to charted safety information and configured monitoring parameters.

Why Safety Parameters Must Be Correct

Safety settings must reflect the vessel's actual operating conditions. Relevant factors include:

  • Vessel draft and expected changes in draft.
  • Under-keel clearance requirements.
  • Water-level and tidal conditions.
  • Squat and vessel speed in shallow water.
  • Charted depth quality and available survey information.
  • Company procedures and applicable navigation requirements.

An incorrectly selected safety contour or depth value can reduce the usefulness of ECDIS warnings. The system can only evaluate information according to the data and parameters available to it.

Critical point: A route-checking function is an aid to the navigator, not a guarantee that a route is safe. It cannot identify every possible danger, especially when chart information is incomplete, outdated, or inaccurate.

05 / Operational Monitoring

Route Monitoring and Navigation Display

After a route has been planned and approved, ECDIS can help the bridge team monitor the vessel's progress and compare its actual movement with the intended route.

Monitoring the Vessel's Position

During route monitoring, the ECDIS display can show the vessel's position in relation to the planned route, charted hazards, and surrounding geographical features.

The navigation team must continuously assess whether the vessel is following the intended track and whether the available position information is reliable.

Display Modes and Information Layers

ECDIS provides different chart display options. These options allow navigators to control the amount and type of information presented on the screen.

  • Display base: Essential chart information that cannot normally be removed.
  • Standard display: A standard set of information intended for routine navigation.
  • All or additional information: Further chart details that can be displayed when required.
  • Information layers: Selected categories of chart objects and supplementary information.

Display settings should support clear interpretation. Removing too much information can hide important details, while displaying too many objects can create visual clutter.

Chart Scale and Overscaling

Chart scale determines the relationship between distances shown on the screen and actual geographical distances. Navigators must use appropriate chart scales for the navigational task.

Overscaling occurs when a chart is enlarged beyond its intended compilation scale. This can make information appear more detailed than the underlying survey and chart data actually support.

Zooming in does not create new survey information or improve the original accuracy of the chart.

Route Monitoring Alerts

Depending on the equipment and configuration, ECDIS may provide alerts related to:

  • Approaching charted dangers.
  • Crossing a configured safety contour.
  • Leaving the planned route or exceeding cross-track limits.
  • Approaching certain restricted or safety-related areas.
  • Position or sensor input problems.
  • Chart data and system status conditions.

Officers must understand what each alert means and whether it requires immediate action. Repeated unnecessary alarms can create alarm fatigue and reduce attention to important warnings.

06 / Practical Applications

ECDIS in Maritime Operations

Electronic charts and ECDIS are used in different maritime environments to support navigation, route assessment, operational awareness, and voyage management.

Commercial Shipping

Supports voyage planning and monitoring for cargo vessels, tankers, container ships, and other commercial vessels.

Passenger Vessels

Helps bridge teams monitor routes, navigational constraints, and the vessel's progress during passenger operations.

Offshore Operations

Provides chart-based geographical awareness around offshore installations, subsea infrastructure, and operational areas.

Port and Harbour Navigation

Supports navigation through channels, harbour approaches, restricted waters, and areas with complex maritime infrastructure.

Integration with Other Bridge Systems

ECDIS is one part of a wider bridge navigation environment. Its information can be used alongside other systems to improve the bridge team's understanding of the vessel's surroundings.

  • Radar: Helps detect and track surrounding targets using radio waves.
  • AIS: Provides identity and voyage-related information transmitted by AIS-equipped targets.
  • GNSS: Supplies satellite-derived position information.
  • Gyrocompass: Supplies heading information for navigation and display alignment.
  • Speed log: Supplies vessel speed measurements.

Radar and AIS overlays can improve situational awareness, but they must be interpreted correctly. AIS information depends on transmitted data and does not replace radar observation or visual lookout.

Practical Scenario: Approaching a Shallow Channel

A cargo vessel is approaching a narrow channel with limited water depth. The bridge team uses ECDIS to review the planned route, examine charted depths, configure safety parameters, and monitor the vessel's position.

During the approach, the system indicates that the vessel is nearing a selected safety boundary. The officer must evaluate the alert, verify the vessel's position and sensor inputs, and take appropriate navigational action.

Learning point: ECDIS supports the decision-making process, but the bridge team remains responsible for interpreting information and maintaining safe navigation.

07 / Technical Challenges

Limitations, Risks and System Reliability

Although ECDIS improves access to navigational information, its effectiveness depends on accurate chart data, reliable sensor inputs, correct configuration, appropriate training, and effective operational procedures.

1. Outdated or Incomplete Chart Data

Electronic charts require updates to reflect changes in navigational conditions. New hazards, changes in depth, modified navigation aids, and updated restricted areas may not appear in an outdated chart database.

Navigators must verify chart coverage and update status before relying on chart information for a voyage.

2. Incorrect Safety Settings

Incorrect safety depths, safety contours, or route-monitoring parameters can result in unsuitable warnings or missed indications.

The selected settings should be reviewed and checked against the vessel's characteristics, voyage conditions, and operating procedures.

3. Sensor Input Errors

ECDIS may display incorrect vessel information when its connected sensors provide inaccurate or unreliable data. For example, a faulty GNSS input may show a misleading vessel position.

Independent position checks and comparison with other available navigation sources remain important.

4. Human Factors and Over-Reliance

Over-reliance on electronic navigation can reduce situational awareness if officers stop cross-checking information or fail to maintain an effective visual lookout.

Proper training, watchkeeping procedures, and regular verification of system information are essential.

5. Display Clutter and Overscaling

Poor display configuration can make important chart information difficult to identify. Excessive zooming can also create a false impression of geographical detail.

6. Cybersecurity Risks

ECDIS is a computer-based navigation system connected to other onboard equipment and information sources. Improper software updates, unauthorized access, infected removable media, or compromised data sources can affect system reliability.

Cybersecurity measures should include controlled access, approved update procedures, software maintenance, network protection, and suitable incident-response plans.

7. GNSS Interference and Position Reliability

GNSS jamming can disrupt satellite signals, while spoofing can provide misleading position or timing information. If ECDIS receives incorrect position data, the displayed vessel location may not represent its true position.

Navigators should use independent cross-checks and follow the vessel's procedures for operating in degraded or GNSS-denied environments.

Key engineering lesson: ECDIS safety depends on the complete information chain: chart production, chart updates, sensor accuracy, system configuration, software reliability, and human decision-making.

08 / Future Developments

The Future of Digital Chart Systems

The development of digital hydrographic standards and integrated maritime information systems is expanding the capabilities of electronic navigation.

1. S-100-Based Navigation

The IHO S-100 framework supports a family of interoperable digital maritime data products. It creates opportunities to use different types of information within compatible navigation systems.

2. Bathymetric Surface Information

More detailed bathymetric information can help provide a better understanding of underwater terrain and depth variations, subject to the quality and availability of the underlying data.

3. Water-Level and Current Information

Digital water-level and surface-current products can support navigation planning by providing additional environmental information.

4. Improved Data Interoperability

Standardized maritime data products can help different navigation and information systems exchange information more consistently.

5. Intelligent Navigation Support

Advanced decision-support systems may combine chart data, sensor information, environmental conditions, and operational constraints to help identify risks and support navigational decisions.

However, automated recommendations must remain transparent, verifiable, and subject to appropriate human oversight. Intelligent systems should support navigators rather than replace professional responsibility.

09 / Knowledge Summary

Key Concepts to Remember

  • Electronic Navigational Charts provide structured digital information about maritime environments.
  • Raster charts are image-based, while vector ENCs store information as geographic objects.
  • ECDIS combines electronic chart data with navigation inputs and software functions.
  • GNSS, gyrocompass, speed log, radar, and AIS can contribute information to the bridge environment.
  • IHO standards define important aspects of chart data, presentation, and protection.
  • Route planning includes hazard assessment, waypoint creation, safety settings, and route checking.
  • Route monitoring helps compare the vessel's movement with its intended route.
  • Chart scale, data quality, and update status affect the reliability of navigation information.
  • Incorrect sensor inputs or safety settings can reduce the effectiveness of ECDIS.
  • Human oversight and independent navigation checks remain essential.

Final concept: ECDIS is not simply an electronic map. It is a safety-related navigation system that integrates chart information, sensor data, monitoring functions, and human decision-making.
